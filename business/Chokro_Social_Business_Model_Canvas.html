Social Business Model Canvas

Chokro — QR-verified recycling rewards and compliance-grade plastic passports · Dhaka pilot, soft launch September 2026
CHOKROAn independent project by Arnish
Version 1.0 · 10 September 2026

Key Resources

  • The Chokro app and backend — 40 features, ~42,800 lines, 1,130 automated tests
  • Chokro's own QR-coded bins at 3–5 anchor wards and campuses
  • The plastic passport ledger — the verified custody record we sell
  • Verification stack: live location check, AI item count and brand-label reading, admin manual review
  • Founder time plus three short freelance engagements (manual review operator, UI/UX, QA)
  • First investment (Brian, Sep 2026); soft-launch budget of about Tk 95,000
  • Access: ward and campus siting permission, DoE registration, collector and recycler MOUs

Partners + Key Stakeholders

  • Collectors and recyclers under MOU — empty the bins, process the plastic, confirm custody
  • Bin stewards: mosque, school and bazaar committees, paid per verified kilogram
  • Ward authorities and campus administrations — permission to site and service bins
  • Department of Environment — EPR registration, annual progress reports, or the PRO route
  • Producers, importers and brand owners — customers and the subjects of the data
  • bKash / Nagad — collector payouts and merchant disbursement
  • Eco-SME sellers (Greenpreneurs); RMG factories and jhut traders on the supply side
  • Cloud and AI vendors; the investor and freelance specialists

Key Activities

  • Install, label and service QR-coded bins; keep the printed layer alive through user reports
  • Verify every submission — geofence, AI screening, escalation to manual review when confidence is low
  • Weigh at collection and apportion mass across the verified drops since the last pickup
  • Issue plastic passports: material type and form, kilograms, brand owner, place, time, how verified, where it went, carbon avoided
  • Sign and manage collector and recycler MOUs; track chain of custody end to end
  • Recruit and onboard eco-SME sellers; operate the discount marketplace
  • Sell EPR compliance reporting to obligated brand owners; support their DoE filings
  • Run sponsor-funded campaigns — a bounded multiplier, on one material, at named bins, in a date window
  • Publish eco-story cards to Chokro's social media with the user's permission
  • Non-programme: registration and licensing, PDPA compliance, budgeting, fundraising, hiring

Type of Intervention

  • A product, not a workshop — and three layers that only work together:
  • Physical: Chokro-owned bins, each with a unique QR code, sited at anchor points
  • Consumer: a free mobile app (Android first) that rewards a verified drop with points
  • Business: the plastic passport — a compliance-grade data service sold to brand owners
  • Marketplace: eco-SME sellers offer 10–50% discounts; users spend or donate their points
  • Delivered as a Dhaka pilot across a few wards and campuses; soft launch end of September 2026

Channels

  • The bin itself — QR code and printed label, with a web fallback for people without the app
  • The Chokro mobile app (Android first; iOS pending a build machine and paid enrolment)
  • Chokro's social media — eco-story photo cards, shared with permission
  • Bin stewards and community committees; campus and ward partners
  • Institutional and ward leaderboards rather than individual ones
  • Direct outreach to brand owners, importers and CSR teams; the DoE registration list
  • Word of mouth inside the seller cohort

Segments

Beneficiary

  • Everyday supporters who drop plastic at a bin and earn points
  • Eco-friendly SME sellers — Greenpreneurs — who list, showcase and promote for free
  • Waste collectors, of whom Dhaka has roughly 100,000, who gain a registered identity, a payment record and a portable work history
  • Dhaka residents and the city itself: less plastic in drains, canals and landfill

Customer

  • Producers, importers and brand owners obligated under the EPR guidelines gazetted 13 August 2026
  • Corporate CSR budgets and sponsors funding the reward pool
  • Advertisers — in the app and at the bins
  • Buyers of graded jhut and recovered-material lots
  • Social-impact investors

Value Proposition

Social Value Proposition

  • Plastic that would have gone to a drain or a landfill is collected, tracked and actually recycled — Chokro owns the chain from bin to recycler
  • Informal collectors are brought into a payment system with a work record instead of staying invisible
  • Small eco-businesses reach an audience already choosing to act, at no cost to them
  • The reward, the recycling and the evidence all come out of the same single action

Impact Measures

  • Kilograms collected and recycled, split across the gazette's five plastic categories
  • Plastic passports issued, and kilograms attributable to each brand owner
  • Carbon avoided, calculated from published emission factors — third-party validation still outstanding
  • Verified drops, active bins, and ward or campus league position
  • Collectors registered, paid, and the kilograms credited to each
  • Sellers onboarded and discounts redeemed

Customer Value Proposition

  • For obligated companies: audit-ready, geofenced, timestamped chain-of-custody evidence for the annual DoE report — and registration renewal depends on that report
  • A report is something you write about yourself; a passport is issued to you by somebody else and stamped by the places you have actually been
  • Goodwill is optional for a company. A legal filing is not — that is why the passport sells and the rewards do not
  • For sponsors: measurable CSR with named bins, a chosen material and a fixed date window
  • For sellers: a free storefront, promotion, and customers who came for the reason the shop exists
  • For collectors: reliable pickups, mobile payout and proof of the work done

Cost Structure

  • Bin fabrication, installation and servicing at each anchor site
  • Manual review labour — a running cost, not a hire: about Tk 0 at pilot (roughly 25 reviews a day, absorbed by the founder), about Tk 40,000 a month at city scale, about Tk 240,000 a month at national scale
  • Cloud, storage and AI screening, charged per verification
  • Collection and recycling logistics under the MOUs
  • Registration and licensing — trade licence, signboard and VAT renew annually, not once
  • Soft launch (one-time plus annual plus one month): Tk 94,577–95,107. First twelve months: Tk 314,508–315,038
  • As it scales: review and verification costs rise with volume, cloud cost per user falls, and bins amortise over years

Surplus

  • Reinvested rather than distributed. In priority order:
  • 1. More bins in more wards, and the two features Bangladesh most needs — Bangla language and offline submission
  • 2. The reward pool and collector payouts, so the collection side stays funded without a sponsor gap
  • 3. Chokro's own eco initiatives — tree planting and similar — which is also where donated points go
  • 4. New verticals: e-waste and batteries, the gazette's sanitary-waste category, and the jhut materials marketplace

Revenue

  • Four engines, pursued in this order of priority. The percentage split is deliberately not fixed yet — the pilot is what will set it:
  • CSR funds — companies paying into the reward pool from existing social budgets
  • Sponsorship — campaigns, in-app placement and branding at the bins
  • EPR data fees — passport and compliance-reporting contracts with obligated brand owners, the only buyer under a legal obligation
  • Marketplace commission on seller transactions, and a jhut / materials marketplace taking both sides of the maker's supply chain
  • Social-impact investment as the funding bridge, not as revenue
  • At the September 2026 pilot: investment and CSR or sponsor commitments only. No EPR contract is signed yet — DoE registration comes first
